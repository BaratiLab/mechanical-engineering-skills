07Design decisions worth copying

A 1:5760 reduction in four meshes
Barrel 80/10, centre 64/8, third 75/10, fourth 84/7. The centre wheel turns once an hour, the fourth once a minute, the escape wheel 12 times a minute, and the barrel once every 8 h.
Every wheel has a job on the dial (hour, minute, seconds) or in the energy budget (8 h per barrel turn gives the 41 h reserve with about 5 spring turns). Pinions of 7-10 leaves keep the wheels small, at the cost of some engagement friction.
The pawl lever winds in both directions with two parts
An eccentric on the rotor pinion rocks a lever with two pawls that push the transmission wheel on both strokes.
Replaces a pair of reverser wheels (each a small clutch of 6-8 parts) and is more efficient at low rotor angles. It is also why this archetype is cheap and robust.
A slipping bridle instead of a stop
The outer end of the mainspring is a friction bridle against the barrel wall.
The automatic can never overwind: at full wind the bridle slips. One feature does the job of a separate torque limiter.
One brass plate locates everything
About 40 bored seats in the main plate hold jewels, pillars and steady pins; the bridges only cap them.
Holding the critical centre distances in one part is what makes the train mesh without fitting by hand. The plate costs more than any wheel because of it.
Water resistance lives in four rubber rings
Crystal, caseback and two crown gaskets are the only seals. The steel and the sapphire do no sealing on their own.
The 200 m rating is a maintenance item, not a property of the case. The rating depends on the cheapest parts in the watch.

12Engineering verdict

A textbook mass-market automatic: the gear train, pawl-lever winding and slipping bridle get the most function from the fewest parts, and the brass main plate does the precision work so that the wheels can be cheap. The cost sits in the case, the crystal and the plate rather than in the gears, and assembly is dominated by tiny-part handling, sixteen movement screws, lubrication and regulation. Reliability is set by the four gaskets, the oil and the hairspring's magnetism, not by the steel. A silicon hairspring and a jam-proof date are the upgrades that change the owner experience; the holder ring and the separate pallet bridge are the cost-downs.
