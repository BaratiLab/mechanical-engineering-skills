07Design decisions worth copying

Clamshell with lay-in architecture
Every internal module drops into the left half; the right half closes it all with 9 screws.
Assembly is top-down in one fixture with no flipping. The cost is that every repair starts by opening the whole tool.
Mixed-material gear train
Plastic ring gear and stage-1 planets, sintered steel stage 2.
Plastic where speed is high and torque is low; steel where torque is high. Noise drops too.
Clutch built into the ring gear
The stage-2 ring gear face carries the ball ramps; the collar only loads the spring.
No separate clutch housing, so fewer parts and a shorter nose.
Shift by locking the carrier
The shift ring slides to lock stage 2 to the housing (low) or to the carrier (high).
Two speeds from one extra part instead of a second gear set.

12Engineering verdict

A well-optimized clamshell: the gearbox and integrated clutch are lean, and plastic is used where it is safe. Assembly is dominated by the harness and the solder joints rather than by the mechanics, and repairability is held back by security screws and by soldered motor leads. Switching to plug-in motor leads and standard screws costs nothing and makes the motor and switch user-replaceable. Brushless is the real next product, not a cost-down.
